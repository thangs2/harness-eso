# Where a document lives — four roots, four jobs

> **Four roots are not four folders of documents.** They are four **different questions**, and a document that answers the
> wrong question is filed in the wrong place no matter how tidy the tree looks.

## 1. One sentence per root

| Root | Answers | Holds | Checked? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **`standard/`** | *"what must be **true**, for **every** project?"* | law | it defines the checks |
| **`sdlc/`** | *"where is **this project**, what was decided, what is missing?"* | evidence | **yes** — by the catalogue |
| **`docs/`** | *"how does a **person** understand and use this product?"* | explanation | **no** |
| **`qc/`** | *"how does a **machine** find out the law was broken?"* | measurement | itself |

**The law is small and the evidence is large, and that ratio is the point.** A standard that grows as large as the project it
governs has stopped being a standard and become another project.

## 2. How they connect

```text
        standard/  ──── produces ────►  sdlc/
          (law)                        (evidence)
            │                              │
            │  defines                     │  is measured
            ▼                              ▼
          qc/  ◄──── compares ─────────────┘
       (machine)

        docs/  ──── explains ────►  a human reader
        (unchecked)
```

One direction only: from law down to evidence. **There is no return path.** If `sdlc/` appears to require a change to
`standard/`, that is not "adjusting the law to reality" — it is a **proposal to change the law**, and it goes through the
decision log.

## 3. Each root, in detail

### `standard/` — law

| May enter | May never enter |
|---|---|
| statements true of **every** project, whatever its language, size, or tooling | a project's name, numbers, or paths |
| a rule and **how to check it** | a rule with no way to be found out |

Reader: whoever writes documents (to know what to write) and the machine (to know what to check).

### `sdlc/` — evidence

| May enter | May never enter |
|---|---|
| anything true **only of this project** | law — see §4 A |
| measurements, decisions, open items, and their dates | a second copy of anything the standard already states |

Reader: the people on the project, and an agent working on it.

### `docs/` — for humans

| May enter | May never enter |
|---|---|
| explanation, examples, guidance, rationale | anything that **must** be true — because nothing checks it |

Reader: whoever uses the product, and whoever joins the work.

### `qc/` — machine

| May enter | May never enter |
|---|---|
| anything a machine can decide | a judgement ("this design is better") |
| one test per catalogue entry | a check no catalogue entry names |

`qc/` holds no prose. It is not documentation; it is measurement. What it asserts must already be law in `standard/06.checks.md`.

## 4. Four laws that keep them apart — each paid for by a real incident

| Law | If broken | What happened here |
|---|---|---|
| **A · Law does not live inside an instance** | consumers receive the gates but not the definition of "documented" | the whole check catalogue sat inside a 401-line project record for weeks |
| **B · A checked root and an unchecked root must be different roots** | every check needs an exception ("except this file"), and exceptions are where things leak | — (avoided; this is why `docs/` was never merged into `sdlc/`) |
| **C · Law is not the machine, and the machine is not the law** | the law is only prose; the machine checks what nobody defined | nine checks were prose; the register listed checks the code did not have |
| **D · One fact, one place** | numbers drift; two places say different things | `339` vs `355` scenarios · `4` vs `25` flow ids · a table heading that disagreed with its own row count |

**Law C has a subtle form.** A definition written in prose can be **wrong** and nothing will say so. Three checks (`X2`, `X6`,
`X13`) described the wrong shape for the thing they claimed to check; it surfaced only when someone tried to write them as code.
A catalogue entry that has never been executed is a sentence, not a check.

## 5. Boundaries that are still open

### a. `OPEN` — where does a document **about the product's model** belong?

`sdlc/03.inception/03.artifact-model/` holds nine documents describing the artifact model **of the product**. They sit in the
instance, but their subject is the product.

> Two answers are both defensible: `standard/` because the model applies to every project, or `docs/` because it explains rather
> than legislates. **Not decided.** Whoever decides must say which of the three questions in §6 the file answers, and write the
> reason here.

### b. `comments/` belongs to none of the four

Dated records of past work. Not law, not evidence, not explanation, not measurement. They are not rewritten and not checked —
a record must keep saying what it said.

### c. `packages/` and `profiles/` are code and data, not documents

`packages/` ships the product. `profiles/` holds product knowledge in machine form (plugin catalogue, standards). Both live
outside the four roots, and neither is governed by the documentation standard.

## 6. Deciding where a file goes — three questions, in this order

```text
1. Is it true of EVERY project?      → standard/
2. Can a machine decide it?          → qc/
3. Must it be true of THIS project?  → sdlc/
   Otherwise                         → docs/
```

**The order matters.** Ask 3 before 1 and everything that "only applies to this project" stays in `sdlc/` — which is exactly
how law came to be forgotten inside an instance for weeks.

## Luật sửa

Wrong when: a rule names a project · a check exists with no catalogue entry · a catalogue entry names no test · the same fact is
stated in two roots · a document in `docs/` is cited as authority for whether something is correct.
