# Section A and Section B — every repo has both, and they answer different questions

> **A — the project.** How *this* repo runs itself: where its artifacts live, what state they are in, what is missing, and the
> checks that verify *its own* tree.
>
> **B — the product.** What this repo **ships to others**: vision, mission, requirements, capabilities, behaviour — everything a
> consumer receives and everything a consumer's user meets.
>
> A repo that never distinguishes the two will eventually decide one using the measurements of the other.

## 1. The test

| Question | Section |
|---|---|
| *"Is **our** tree complete / consistent / up to date?"* | **A** |
| *"What does the product **do**, for whom, and how is it measured?"* | **B** |
| *"How many files do **we** have?"* | **A** — a count of our tree is not a fact about the product |
| *"What must a project declare about its artifacts?"* | **B** — a rule for consumers is not a fact about our tree |

## 2. Rules

1. **Every artifact serves exactly one section.** Cross-references are fine; shared ownership is not. An artifact serving both
   will be changed for one reason and break the other.
2. **A governs this repo, and nowhere else.** A consumer does not receive our registers, our status, our WBS.
3. **B is what is replicated.** A consumer receives the product — provisioned structure plus behaviour. B is written *for them*,
   so it may not name our files, our counts, or our incidents as if they were theirs.
4. **A measurement of A is not evidence about B.** "0 of 386 documents declare `x`" says something about our tree; it says nothing
   about whether `x` should be a rule.
5. **B is not a snapshot of A.** If the product's definition is whatever our repo happens to look like, it is a description of us
   wearing the product's name.

## 3. The failure this prevents — recorded, because it happened three times here

| Occasion | What was conflated | Result |
|---|---|---|
| deciding facet cost | counted **this tree's** 386 documents to price a rule meant for **consumers** | the cost was real, the question was wrong |
| the "five-law package" | three rules of **product semantics** + two items of **our own data** | nothing to accept or reject — two different decisions |
| template work | `standard/templates/library.json` used as **our** self-audit, while templates are a **product** artifact | useful, but reported as if it were B when it was A |

The tell is always the same: the question *"adopt or not?"* has no single answer, because the thing being adopted lives in two
places.

## 4. How a consumer sees it

```text
Their repo                        Our repo
  Section A  ← theirs, theirs to run        Section A  ← ours
  Section B  ← the product we ship ────────▶ Section B  ← ours
```

They own their A and receive our B. Which means: **anything in our B that depends on our A is a defect**, and anything in our A
that we try to make others obey is a rule we did not write down as one.

## Luật sửa

Wrong when: an artifact is cited for a decision in the wrong section · a B document counts our files as evidence · an A document
imposes an obligation on a consumer · one artifact is claimed by both sections.
